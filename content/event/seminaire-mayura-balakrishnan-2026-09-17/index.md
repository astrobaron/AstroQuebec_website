## Titre

Dissecting the High Energy Environment of Sgr A* in X-rays and IR

## Résumé

The Galactic Center (GC) is a uniquely complex high-energy laboratory where the low-luminosity supermassive black hole Sgr A*, the supernova remnant Sgr A East, shocked stellar winds, molecular material, and diffuse X-ray emission overlap along the line of sight. In this talk, I will present two studies that use X-ray and infrared observations to separate and interpret this crowded environment. First, I will show how Poissonian Generalized Morphological Component Analysis applied to deep Chandra imaging isolates the first uncontaminated X-ray maps of Sgr A East, revealing its Fe-rich core, surrounding S/Ar/Ca-enhanced plasma, and interaction with dense Galactic Center material. Second, I will discuss JWST/MIRI time-resolved spectroscopy of Sgr A*, which tests whether mid-infrared emission lines can constrain otherwise inaccessible UV flare emission. The absence of detectable line variability, supported by CLOUDY modeling, shows that the circumnuclear gas responds too slowly to trace individual flares, highlighting the limits of infrared line diagnostics as probes of Sgr A*'s UV output. Together, these studies sharpen our understanding of the Sgr A* environment by isolating its neighboring plasma structures and identifying how, and on what timescales, the surrounding gas records activity from the central black hole.
