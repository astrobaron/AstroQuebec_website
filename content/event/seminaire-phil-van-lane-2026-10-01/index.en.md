## Title

From Stars to Statistics: using data-driven variability models to constrain stellar ages

## Abstract

Stellar ages are a foundational component of astronomy research, but are notoriously difficult to measure for low mass main sequence stars. Stellar variability indicators provide some constraints, but they evolve in nonlinear and non-monotonic ways. My research takes advantage of the recent rapid growth of both astronomy survey data and computing capabilities, by applying ML/AI and statistical techniques to study stellar variability and its applicability to constraining stellar ages. In this talk I will present two data-driven models that I have developed. The first is ChronoFlow: an ML-based stellar rotation model that provides probabilistic age estimates. The second is EncoTESS: a Time Series Foundation Model that uses raw time series data to estimate stellar ages. In this talk I will discuss the age inference capabilities of these models, the implications of these techniques to the study of variability in low mass stars, and ideas for how these lines of research can be expanded in the future.
