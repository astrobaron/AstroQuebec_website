## Title

Magnetic interactions around cool stars: grazing comets and hot spot formation

## Abstract

The presence of close-in exoplanets has been shown to impact the stellar flaring rate (Ilin et al. 2025) and tracers of stellar magnetic activity (e.g. Shkolnik et al. 2008), thought to be mainly caused by star-planet magnetic interactions (SPMI). In these interactions, Alfvén waves can propagate from the exoplanet back towards the star and deposit energy in the stellar atmosphere, forming localized hotspots (Strugarek et al. 2025) or potentially triggering eruptions akin to sympathetic flaring on the Sun (e.g., Guité et al. 2025). It is often said that these signals could allow us to place constraints on planetary magnetic fields, but these constraints depend on the interpretive model: are we dealing with a direct deposition of energy, or is the SPMI simply a triggering phenomenon? It is today not known if the two scenarios co-exist, or if one of these scenarios is actually enough to explain all phenomena classically attributed to SPMIs. In this talk, I will present my recent work addressing both energy deposition scenarios. First, I will introduce the first tentative detection of SPMI in our solar system, where a solar eruption appears to have been triggered by the magnetic interaction between the Sun and comet Lovejoy during its perihelion passage through the solar corona (Guité et al. 2026). I will discuss the energetics of this event and how solar system analogs inform our understanding of SPMI in stellar and exoplanetary systems. Second, I will present the first 3D numerical simulations of hotspot formation driven by Alfvén waves in a stellar atmosphere via SPMI, focusing on wave dynamics and energy deposition. Finally, I will demonstrate how this energy deposition impacts synthetic spectral lines, providing a crucial diagnostic to compare against observations.
