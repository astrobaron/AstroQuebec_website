## Titre

Towards Arrays with 10,000+ Antenna Elements: Enabling Technologies for Wide-Field Radio Astronomy

## Résumé

Large arrays of small antennas can combine high sensitivity with enormous fields of view, opening new opportunities in fast radio transient detection, pulsar astronomy, and studies of astrophysical plasmas. However, scaling these arrays to tens of thousands of elements multiplies every dollar, watt, and cable required per antenna. Drawing on my work leading the signal-chain development for the Canadian Hydrogen Observatory and Radio-transient Detector (CHORD) and the Canadian–Chilean Array for Radio Transient Studies (CHARTS), I will discuss approaches to reducing the cost and complexity of antennas, analog electronics, and digitizers. Examples range from a receiver chain costing less than US$100 per antenna to a new digitizer design based on a chip originally developed for 5G networks. I will also present recent results from SimpleClock, a low-cost satellite-disciplined timing system, and explore how distributed architectures with independently clocked subarrays could allow the next generation of wide-field radio telescopes to grow in stages.
