## Titre

How JWST Luminosities Constrain the Local Mass Function and the Smallest Products of Star Formation

## Résumé

The understanding of the life cycle of stars developed by 20th century astronomers rested on their ability to measure fundamental properties such as mass, radius, bolometric luminosity, effective temperature, and the mass function. 21st century astronomers have embarked on a similar quest to understand the evolution of brown dwarfs but have been hampered by their intrinsic faintness, the lack of a mass-luminosity relation, and the fact that most of their radiation is emitted at infrared wavelengths. While progress has been made in understanding the hotter brown dwarfs that populate the L and T spectral classes, the cooler brown dwarfs in the Y class have only become reachable with JWST. In this talk, I will present our work using the first 5 years of JWST data to create the most precise luminosity function of the local substellar neighborhood. By performing MCMC fits on this luminosity function, we place constraints on the local mass and age functions, finding a mass function similar to previous works and a slightly young distribution. Our age distribution is notably discrepant with the extremely young age distribution reported by Best et al. 2024, the last attempt to probe the local mass function. In fitting the luminosity function, we are also able to empirically constrain the effective temperature where the cloudy-to-clear transition occurs in evolutionary models, finding a temperature hotter than assumed by the most recent hybrid evolutionary models. As caution for future works and to improve our confidence in our results, we for the first time test for the bias caused how the luminosity function is binned, finding fitting different bin sizes can significantly alter fit parameters. We account for this bias by fitting multiple binnings and combining their posteriors to arrive at a more robust understanding of the mass and age distribution of the local brown dwarf population.
