## Research project

Daryl Haggard is a specialist in observational astronomy. She and her team are studying Sagittarius A*, the supermassive black hole at the centre of our galaxy, the Milky Way. Sgr A* has a mass 4 million times that of the Sun and is 100 times closer than any other supermassive black hole. Its proximity allows us to see hot matter (100 million °C) falling towards the black hole’s event horizon. Daryl Haggard’s team uses telescopes covering several wavelength ranges, from the radio band through to the submillimetre, near-infrared and X-ray bands, to probe the exotic environment of Sgr A*, where strong gravity plays a key role. Her team is also studying the interaction between supermassive black holes – or active galactic nuclei – and their distant host galaxies. And, when the opportunity arises, she and her team attempt to detect the X-rays emitted by neutron stars and black holes when they collide. These collisions produce ripples through space-time – gravitational waves – which have now been detected by LIGO and Virgo.
