## Research project

Tracy Webb’s research focuses on the growth of structure in the universe, and in galaxies in particular. Her approach involves using data obtained at different wavelengths; each wavelength probes a different physical process and provides insight into a unique aspect of galaxy formation. Since many of the physical phenomena in galaxies take place behind thick veils of dust, her research focuses on observations in the sub-millimetre (~400–1200 μm) and mid-infrared (~3–400 μm) regions, which allow the dust to be detected directly and provide clues as to what is happening behind it. She mainly studies galaxies in the early, distant universe (i.e. at high redshifts) because, due to the finite speed of light, we see these systems as they existed 5 to 12 billion years ago. We can therefore literally watch them forming! She is also launching programmes to study neighbouring galaxies, as these systems can be studied in much greater detail and will provide insight into the processes that formed today’s galaxies.
