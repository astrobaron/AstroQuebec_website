## Research project

Gilles Joncas’s research focuses on the study of each of the components of the interstellar medium (ionised, atomic and molecular gas, and dust) across a wide range of wavelengths, from the visible to the centimetre range. The aim is to gain a comprehensive (integrated) understanding in order to develop an evolutionary scenario for the various objects analysed and to gain insight into the physical interactions between the different components. More specifically, the work of Gilles Joncas and his collaborators focuses on: 1) the kinematics of ionised gas, primarily using the FaNTOMM instrument on the telescope at the Mont-Mégantic Observatory; 2) mapping the temperature and density of this same gas using SpIOMM and FaNTOMM; 3) the kinematics (overall motions and turbulence) of neutral gas (HI) at high galactic latitudes, based on data from the International Galactic Plane Survey (IGPS), using the DRAO, VLA and AT radio telescopes, as well as data obtained at the Green Bank Telescope; 4) the characterisation of the HI medium using statistical and topological tools; 5) the evolution of dust grains in molecular cloud formation regions and within molecular clouds, both with and without star formation, using the SCUBA2 survey from the James Clerk Maxwell Telescope, the HiGAL survey from the Herschel Space Telescope, and the all-sky survey from the Planck satellite.
