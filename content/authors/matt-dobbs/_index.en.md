## Research project

Matt Dobbs’s research aims to improve our understanding of the fundamental building blocks of the universe, including its origin, history and fate, as well as to provide new insights into the early universe, where the laws of particle physics and cosmology intersect. His group builds innovative instruments and experiments to explore the early stages of the universe through observations of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) in the millimetre range, as well as radio observations of hydrogen emission lines. The polarisation of the CMB is one of the few known tools for probing physics at the energy scales of the universe’s inflationary era, where grand unified theories may manifest themselves. At McGill, Matt Dobbs and his group are building the sampling system for new experiments that measure the polarisation of the CMB and which could reveal the signature of gravitational waves from the inflationary era. Another fundamental unknown is the nature of dark energy, which currently accounts for around 70 per cent of the energy density in the universe. Matt Dobbs is involved in the conception, design, construction and scientific operation of the CHIME telescope, which will measure large-scale structure by mapping the 21 cm emission at redshifts ranging from 0.8 to 2.5. CHIME will contribute to a better understanding of the history of the universe’s expansion and provide new insights into dark energy.
