## Research project

René Doyon’s research focuses on the development of cutting-edge astronomical instrumentation for various ground-based and space-based observatories. He is also actively involved in various observation programmes aimed at researching and characterising brown dwarfs, exoplanets and low-mass young stars. On the instrumentation front, René Doyon is leading various projects involving infrared instrumentation (cameras and spectrographs) for the Mont-Mégantic Observatory telescope. He is also involved in the development of the Gemini Planet Imager for the Gemini South telescope in Chile, which has been in operation since 2013. He is the co-principal investigator of SPIRou, a high-resolution spectro-polarimeter optimised for the infrared spectrum, for the Canada-France-Hawaii Telescope. SPIRou is designed to detect terrestrial (i.e. Earth-like) planets located in the ‘habitable zone’ around low-mass stars in the solar neighbourhood. René Doyon is also the principal investigator for NIRISS, one of the four scientific instruments on the James Webb Space Telescope.
