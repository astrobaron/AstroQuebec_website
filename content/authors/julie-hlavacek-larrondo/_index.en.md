## Research project

Julie Hlavacek-Larrondo conducts cutting-edge research in high-energy astrophysics and extragalactic astrophysics. The main aim of her work is to understand the impact of supermassive black holes on their surrounding environment and, ultimately, to identify their role in the formation and evolution of galaxies. The accretion of matter onto a supermassive black hole at the centre of a galaxy often gives it remarkable properties. For instance, it can become so bright that its luminosity eventually exceeds that of the galaxy itself; furthermore, extremely powerful jets of relativistic particles can extend over phenomenal distances, often far greater than the size of the galaxy itself. Black holes therefore have an undeniable impact on the final properties of their surrounding environment. Despite their importance, the details of the role played by black holes remain poorly understood. The original research projects led by Julie Hlavacek-Larrondo aim to significantly improve our understanding of these objects. To achieve this, she uses data obtained with the world’s best telescopes, including the Chandra X-ray Observatory, the new Very Large Array, the Hubble Space Telescope and the Gemini observatories.
