## Research project

Laurent Drissen is a specialist in massive stars. For the past fifteen years or so, he has been working on the development of Fourier-transform imaging spectrometers (iFTS), instruments capable of obtaining the spectrum of each light source within a field of view of 11 arcminutes, across selected wavelength ranges in the visible spectrum. Together with numerous students and industry partners (in particular ABB in Quebec City), he led the design and construction of SpIOMM for the Mont-Mégantic Observatory. More recently, he oversaw the development of SITELLE, an instrument similar to SpIOMM, which is now installed on the Canada-France-Hawaii Telescope. Using SITELLE, Drissen and his collaborators are studying ionised gas nebulae in the Milky Way, nearby spiral galaxies and distant galaxy clusters. Laurent Drissen is also active in the field of science communication; for many years he was a regular contributor to Radio-Canada’s programme ‘Les années lumière’.
