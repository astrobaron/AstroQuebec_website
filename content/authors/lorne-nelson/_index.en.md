## Research project

The main focus of Lorne Nelson’s research is the theoretical study of the structure, formation and evolution of low-mass and intermediate-mass stars in close binary systems, where one of the two components is a compact object: a white dwarf, a neutron star or a black hole. The most important scientific objective is the development of a coherent description of the formation and evolution of interacting binary systems. Most stars reside in ‘extended’ binary systems, but some of the most significant and exotic phenomena are observed in close, interacting binary systems. These range from binary systems containing a black hole to contact binaries. These systems offer valuable insights into physics (for example, radiative hydrodynamics, runaway thermonuclear bursts, and the properties of compact matter) and can be used as laboratories in which we can test the predictions of a wide range of fields, such as general relativity and high-energy physics. A better understanding of the properties and evolution of these stars provides a unique window into the age, scale and structure of the Universe.
