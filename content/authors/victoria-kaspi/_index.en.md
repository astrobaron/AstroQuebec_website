## Research project

Victoria Kaspi’s research focuses on neutron stars: ultra-dense, rapidly rotating stars that are close relatives of black holes. Her research aims to constrain fundamental physics by observing neutron stars, as their extreme physical properties are of great interest but cannot be simulated in a laboratory. To this end, she observes neutron stars using the largest and most powerful radio telescopes and X-ray telescopes currently in operation. Among the specific questions she hopes to answer are: how neutron stars are formed, how fast they can rotate, what they are made of, and what kind of magnetic fields they harbour. These questions place constraints on fundamental problems such as the equation of state of dense matter and the physics of supernova explosions, the source of the matter from which we are made. She also uses binary pulsars to study a variety of topics ranging from theories of gravity to the evolution of binary systems and stellar structure.
