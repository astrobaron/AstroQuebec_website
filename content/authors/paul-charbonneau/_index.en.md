## Research project

The Sun’s magnetic activity cycle is both the driving force and the energy source behind all eruptive phenomena that have an impact on Earth, whether in terms of space weather, damage to technological infrastructure, or potential influence on long-term climate variations. The research carried out by Paul Charbonneau and his team aims, amongst other things, to improve our understanding of the physical mechanisms underlying this magnetic cycle, and of the significant fluctuations observed in its amplitude and duration. The unifying physical principle underlying all the phenomena being modelled is the non-linear interaction between the Sun’s magnetic field and the fluid flows present in its outer layers. Paul Charbonneau and his team have recently achieved a world first: the development of a magnetohydrodynamic numerical simulation of solar convection that produces a magnetic field on large spatial scales, and whose spatiotemporal evolution resembles the solar cycle in many respects. This includes, in particular, regular reversals of magnetic polarity on a multi-decade timescale.
