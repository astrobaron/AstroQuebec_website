## Research project

Patrick Dufour’s research focuses primarily on the study of the atmospheres of white dwarf stars, drawing on both theoretical approaches (detailed calculations of atmospheric models) and observational data (spectroscopy and photometry). White dwarfs are the remnants of low-mass stars that have exhausted their supply of nuclear fuel. A typical white dwarf consists of a core of carbon and oxygen, accounting for over 99 per cent of its mass, surrounded by a layer of helium which, in around 80 per cent of cases, is itself surrounded by a further layer of hydrogen. These layers, although thin, are optically opaque and regulate the star’s rate of energy loss (i.e. its cooling rate). To fully understand the evolution of white dwarfs, it is therefore essential to have a thorough understanding of the physical properties of these surface layers. Spectroscopic analysis of the light emanating from the atmospheres of white dwarfs is the main technique used to obtain information about the outer layers of white dwarfs. Patrick Dufour’s research focuses primarily on analysing the properties of stars showing traces of heavy elements (spectral types DZ and DQ) as well as stars with carbon-rich atmospheres.
