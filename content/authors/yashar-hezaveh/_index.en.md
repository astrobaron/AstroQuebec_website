## Research project

Yashar Hezaveh’s research focuses on the use of strong gravitational lenses to study the nature of dark matter, as well as the formation of the first galaxies in our universe. His research is heavily computational: it involves developing methods for numerical analysis and simulation. More specifically, he is conducting work on the application of machine learning methods to the analysis of astrophysical data. Yashar Hezaveh leads the South Pole Telescope Dark Matter Substructure (SPT DMS) collaboration, within which he is attempting to determine the temperature of dark matter. To do this, he uses observations from the ALMA interferometer (the world’s largest observatory operating at millimetre wavelengths) to detect the smallest dark matter halos in distant galaxies. He also uses these observations to study the most distant galaxies in our universe, in order to understand how the first galaxies in our universe formed.
