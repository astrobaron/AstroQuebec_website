## Research project

Andrew Cumming’s research focuses on the physics of neutron stars and exoplanets, in both cases with the aim of developing theoretical models that can be used to interpret observations and understand the physical processes occurring within these objects. His work on the physics of neutron stars includes thermonuclear burning, magnetic field evolution and the properties of dense matter. He is particularly intrigued by transient phenomena in accretion onto neutron stars and magnetars as a means of probing these various physical phenomena. In the field of exoplanets, he seeks to establish links between the observed properties of exoplanets and theories of their structure, formation and evolution. More recently, he has worked on various aspects of the evolution of gas giant planets, including hot Jupiters and the formation of gas giants in the context of direct imaging of these planets. He has also worked on statistical methods for inferring planetary properties from radial velocity data and statistical studies of the exoplanet population.
