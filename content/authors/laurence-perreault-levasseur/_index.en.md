## Research project

Laurence Perreault-Levasseur’s research focuses on cosmology and, in particular, the development of new machine learning techniques for data analysis and the constraining of cosmological parameters. Although the concordance model in cosmology – known as the standard model of cosmology or the Lambda CDM inflationary model – has been hugely successful in predicting a large number of observable quantities across a wide range of timescales and spatial scales (details of the properties of the cosmic microwave background, such as its power spectrum; the distribution of large-scale structures in the universe; the baryon acoustic oscillation; the expansion of the universe, etc.), the nature of its three main components remains elusive. Over the next decade, a large number of new observatories and major sky surveys will become operational in an attempt to shed light on the nature and properties of these mysterious components: the nature of the field responsible for inflation, dark matter and dark energy. Laurence Perreault-Levasseur’s research focuses on developing new analysis methods based on machine learning to process the unprecedented volume of observations that will be generated by these new experiments. She is particularly interested in how the gravitational lensing effect and the current generation of sky surveys can help us to better constrain its properties. She is a member of the LSST DESC, SLSC and Euclid collaborations.
